The four required objectives

Challenge objectiveWhat the application does
Select Earth-science variables@@DATASET_COUNT@@ curated data series, a plain definition of each variable and question-led cards. Variables represent vegetation, surface temperature, air temperature, precipitation, soil moisture and global temperature anomaly.
Acquire data for a particular regionAdapters ingest MODIS through the Planetary Computer COG mirror, NASA POWER, GPCP and NASA GISTEMP. Regional NetCDF cubes are available for prepared regions; bounded requests can download new regional data. A visitor does not need an Earthdata account for these implemented adapters.
Generate and visualise a time seriesMonthly series, calendar-month anomalies, an optional 12-month running mean, fitted trend and 95% slope uncertainty. The user chooses a region, subarea, date range and months or season.
Estimate the rate and test significanceOLS slopes per decade, memory checks at monthly and yearly scales, confidence intervals, dependence-corrected Mann–Kendall tests, seasonal Sen slope, and Benjamini–Hochberg FDR on map cells. A non-significant interval is not described as proof of no change.

Background and optional considerations

Point in the challengeCoverage and interpretation
What, where, how much, and significanceVariable cards explain what is measured; the map locates the change; slopes and intervals quantify it; the verdict and cross-checks describe the evidence.
Regional changes can have opposite signsRegional maps and presets expose spatial differences. The recorded daytime LST cases show opposite signs in Central Europe and north-west India. Such differences do not establish causes and MODIS caveats still apply.
Land, ocean and atmospheric contextLand variables and near-surface atmospheric reanalysis are implemented. GISTEMP combines land stations with sea-surface observations globally. Dedicated ocean-depth products, pressure-level selection, evaporation and streamflow are outside the current implementation.
Example missions and modelsMODIS Terra/Aqua and MERRA-2 are used analytically. GPM IMERG is offered as contextual imagery, not the precipitation-trend input. OMI, AIRS and GLDAS are examples in the brief, not mandatory products and not implemented adapters here.
High-school, university and public audiencesPlain definitions, question-led selection, explanatory answers, “How we know”, a synthetic false-alarm lab and inspectable methods support these audiences. Classroom learning outcomes have not yet been evaluated.
Question-led discovery and demonstrations@@DATASET_COUNT@@ ready-made questions and prepared regions start an investigation. This deck includes a recorded real result plus three inspectable teaching examples on slide 4. The current app provides runnable noise and two-spike outlier examples.
Optional AI-assisted data discoveryThe current investigation is based on a curated catalogue, not news or literature search. Explanatory answers are fixed templates filled with computed values; optional AI-based discovery is not needed for the implemented workflow.
Temporal resolutions and long enough recordsThe prototype analyses monthly values. Its catalogue explicitly records product periods. Missing-data thresholds and period checks precede interpretation; monthly deseasonalisation accounts for the regular annual cycle.
Non-significant results can be usefulThe interface can return “No clear trend” and the answer explains an interval including zero. Longer records may help but are never presented as a guarantee of significance.
Nonlinear trends and seasonalityLinear trends meet the starting scope of the brief. The period matrix and season filters test sensitivity; the synthetic spike example shows that unusual points can influence the fitted slope. This is not a general nonlinear model or an automatic change-point detector.
Optional external hydrology toolsCUAHSI and the other supplied resources informed the brief; they are not integrated as analytical services. All resources supplied in the local research folder remain separate from implemented datasets.

Implemented application inventory

Globe explorer

  • MapLibre 3D globe, NASA GIBS Blue Marble and historical imagery, Natural Earth borders, and a Yale Bright Star Catalogue background.
  • Imagery choices: true colour, monthly vegetation, monthly land-surface heat, monthly air temperature and daily IMERG rainfall. These are contextual layers; they are not interchangeable with the analytical data sources.
  • Trend maps, annual anomaly frames, play/pause through years, coordinated imagery date and month selection, data opacity and data visibility controls.
  • FDR-based fading, cell-level hover statistics, cell clicks, world/region navigation and separate global-monthly visual layers for datasets without a global statistical cube. Global GISTEMP can also find and save non-overlapping areas with similar average mapped trend rates; this compares rates, not whole climates.
  • Rectangle selection, dataset-specific extent limits, reuse of existing cubes, asynchronous regional downloads with progress, result cards and a mini time-series chart.
  • “Full analysis” carries the selected area into Detective.
  • New areas are recorded on the server; each visitor controls which saved places are visible. Other visitors’ areas can be shown, and hiding a place does not delete it for everyone.
  • Tile/frame prefetch, in-place image changes, fading between imagery dates and softened polar imagery support a smooth visual experience.

Detective

  • @@DATASET_COUNT@@ question cards with definitions; a catalogue of prepared and available custom regions; dataset availability checks.
  • Start/end-year dropdowns and timeline controls, all-year and seasonal selections, clicked grid cells and rectangle subareas.
  • A trend map with a physical-unit scale, missing-data indication, FDR fading and a separate valid-month coverage view.
  • Plain-language answers grounded in the computed result: trend direction and rate, overall change, ordinary year-to-year variability, spatial distribution, sensitivity and a source-specific caveat.
  • Verdicts distinguish detected trends, an interval including zero, period-dependent direction, period-dependent significance and insufficient data.
  • OLS interval and p-value from monthly and yearly memory checks, corrected Mann–Kendall, Sen slope, sample sizes, valid data share and overlapping-window sensitivity.
  • Monthly anomaly and running-mean charts, fitted trend and confidence fan; a typical seasonal-cycle chart.
  • An interactive start/end-year matrix. Clicking a cell updates the selected period.
  • Cross-source comparisons on common areas and periods: POWER/GISTEMP temperature, POWER/GPCP rainfall and Terra/Aqua surface temperature; identification of the largest mapped warming rate in the relevant answer.
  • Source product, citation, provider, resolution, licence and caveats visible alongside the analysis.
  • CSV time-series export, JSON evidence export and a reproducible URL hash for the variable, region, period, months and subarea.

False-alarm laboratory

  • Clearly labelled synthetic AR(1) monthly data with no underlying continuous trend; controls for years and memory; new seeded examples.
  • Naive and corrected significance tests with the same observed synthetic series, so users can see the role of persistence.
  • A 200-run experiment with counts, proportions, 95% Wilson intervals, and three bars: naive result, corrected result and the fixed nominal 5% reference.
  • Two deliberately injected late spikes, generation of new examples and comparison with the original series. The lesson demonstrates OLS sensitivity to known synthetic outliers, without recommending automatic removal of real extremes.
  • Source-specific caution around Amazon precipitation is shown in the real analysis, separately from the simulations.

Resilience and rollout status

The current local build assembles air temperature, land surface temperature, precipitation and root-zone soil wetness for a common area and period. It supports CSV/JSON export, a printable brief and opening the same selection in Detective. Its use cases cover civilian and defence infrastructure. This is historical regional exposure, not a facility risk score or operational forecast.

Development roadmap

A guided tour is being developed separately; an integrated tour, fire-history analysis, daily heat metrics and a space-weather panel are not claimed as completed presentation features.

Access, experience and engineering

  • mobile layouts, light and dark themes, keyboard controls, reduced-motion support and one tooltip style. A formal screen-reader audit has not been completed.
  • Python/FastAPI API; NumPy, SciPy and xarray calculations; NetCDF regional caches; source adapters; statistical and API tests; localisation regression checks.
  • The browser frontend uses JavaScript, MapLibre, Leaflet and Plotly without a build step.
  • Public demo mode applies job and simulation limits. Optional pruning bounds retained custom data; it is disabled by default locally.
  • Answers are deterministic templates, not generated scientific findings. Every displayed number is calculated from a cited dataset or explicitly synthetic experiment.
Not claimed: GPU acceleration, full global coverage for every variable, automatic causal attribution, operational forecasting, a pressure-level explorer, automatic change-point discovery, measured classroom impact or a completed external scientific validation.

Products, credits and scientific limits

Important limitations. MODIS values are read at native resolution and screened with pixel reliability / QC_Day before aggregation to 0.1°. NDVI screening retains good and marginal pixels. Composite-to-month boundaries remain approximate, and satellite orbit drift after 2022 can affect interpretation. POWER uses MERRA-2 reanalysis; its variables share a model source. GPCP adds a coarse 2.5° satellite–gauge rainfall comparison, with some shared observational inputs. GISTEMP uses spatial smoothing. The selected-period anomaly baseline is not a universal fixed reference period. Statistical significance alone neither validates a source artefact nor identifies a cause.

A short, reproducible live demonstration

  1. Open the live app in English. On Globe, inspect the GISTEMP global trend and its physical-unit legend.
  2. Open Detective, select “Warmer air?”, Central Europe, 1981–2025, all months. Inspect the slope, 95% interval and “How we know”.
  3. Inspect the start/end matrix: @@AIR_SAME@@ of @@AIR_WINDOWS@@ windows have the same direction; @@AIR_SIGNIFICANT_WINDOWS@@ have that direction and p < 0.05.
  4. Open False alarms. Set 10 years and memory 0.7; run 200 series. The recorded seed-1000 experiment produced @@NAIVE_COUNT@@ naive alarms and @@CORRECTED_COUNT@@ corrected alarms. These are simulation outcomes, not Earth observations.
  5. In “Can two spikes tilt a trend?”, compare without spikes. The current recorded example moves from @@SPIKE_WITH@@ to @@SPIKE_WITHOUT@@ synthetic units per decade when the known injected spikes are removed.
  6. Return to Globe, draw a small area already inside Central Europe, choose air temperature and analyse. Open the full investigation, then export CSV/JSON.
